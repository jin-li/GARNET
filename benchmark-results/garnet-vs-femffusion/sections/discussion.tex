\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Where the time goes.} The two codes spend their effort in
different places:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{p{4.0cm}p{5.0cm}p{5.9cm}}
\hline
 & \ffn{} & \garnet{} \\
\hline
Value of one unknown & high: degree-3 elements & low: second-order finite volumes \\
Outer iterations to converge & 4--45 (Krylov eigensolvers) & 28--57 (2-group, power iteration); 10 (HP-MR, Wielandt shift) \\
Solve cost per unknown, $\ge 10^6$ unknowns & 36--325~$\mu$s (one core) & 7.5--64~$\mu$s (one core); 0.09--0.9~$\mu$s (GH200) \\
Cost per unknown and inner iteration & -- & 5--32~ns (one core); 0.03--0.43~ns (GH200) \\
Memory per unknown & 2--3.6~kB (process) & 0.2--1.4~kB (CPU); 0.24--2.2~kB (GH200 pool) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Steady, one core:} \ffn's richer unknowns and stronger eigensolver
  outweigh \garnet's cheaper unknowns, by one to two orders of magnitude in
  unknowns at the same error.
\item \textbf{Steady, GH200:} a \garnet{} solve there is 190--320 times
  faster than on one core from 4 million unknowns up (26--110 times at 1 to
  3 million, where the GPU is not yet full). That carries it past \ffn{} on
  one core wherever its discretization reaches the target.
\item \textbf{Transient:} time integration decides the comparison, not space.
  \garnet's second-order BDF against \ffn's first-order semi-implicit schemes is
  worth a factor of 10 on the same core at $10^{-3}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What would change the picture.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A parallel \ffn.} Its MPI path, once repaired (Section~\ref{sec:codes}),
  would divide its times by up to the parallel efficiency times the number
  of cores. At 64 cores and 50\% efficiency, a factor of 32 would bring its
  HP-MR time at 10~pcm from 89~s to about 3~s, close to the GH200's 2.4~s.
  This is an estimate, not a measurement: the repair is deferred.
\item \textbf{\garnet's outer acceleration.} The Wielandt shift written for
  this comparison took the HP-MR from about 250 Anderson-accelerated power
  iterations to 10 shifted solves, 2.2--3.5 times faster
  (Table~\ref{tab:wielandt}). Its shifted solves are preconditioned by a
  fine-mesh group sweep only. \garnet{} still has no CMFD-style coarse-mesh
  correction, which would cut the FGMRES iterations within each solve. On the
  two-group cores the power iteration remains its fastest option, at 28--57
  outers against \ffn's 4--8.
\item \textbf{\garnet's discretization.} A higher-order or nodal-like
  discretization would close the steady gap per core. The node powers at the
  fuel--reflector corners are where the finite volumes lose the most.
\item \textbf{\ffn's time integration.} A working Implicit-Exponential scheme,
  or any second-order scheme, would shrink the transient gap. Its default
  scheme is first order and, on this input, does not hold equilibrium.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{\ffn{} findings.} These were found while posing identical problems,
and could be reported upstream:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Implicit-Exponential takes material~0's decay constants and delayed
  spectra for the whole core. With multi-material kinetics it also drifts
  from equilibrium in proportion to $\Delta t$ (3.9\% in a 0.1~s step).
\item An XML material's prompt spectrum stays zero unless the material has a
  \code{ChiD} element. With kinetics from \code{PREC\_Filename}, the
  Implicit-Exponential transient collapses.
\item \code{full\_allocated} with \code{gd} or \code{bifpam} gives a wrong $k$ on
  the refined Langenbuch core: 1.70 and 1.38 instead of 0.9995.
\item \code{full\_matrixfree} with \code{gd} or \code{slepc\_2g} crashes.
\item Running on more than one MPI rank is not possible yet
  (Section~\ref{sec:codes}).
\end{enumerate}
Items 1--2 are close to open fix branches of the \code{ondrejch} fork
(\code{fix/time-delayed-spectrum-implicit},
\code{fix/xml-default-delayed-spectra}).

\paragraph{Fairness and scope.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Both codes ran the same problems on the same CPU type, each in its best
  measured configuration, at tolerances chosen by the same rule.
\item The GH200 is compared with one EPYC core. That is \ffn's only working
  mode today, not a node-to-node comparison.
\item The steady 1~pcm, 0.1\% target sits within a factor of three of the
  references' resolution (0.3--0.4~pcm). It ranks the platforms only coarsely.
\item The HP-MR is drums-withdrawn. The inserted, area-smeared drums are a
  near-black absorber that neither code resolves (Section~\ref{sec:problems}).
\end{itemize}
